NLP TOPICS – SIMPLE DOCUMENTATION
====================================

1. TOKENIZATION
----------------
Tokenization is the process of breaking text into smaller pieces called tokens.

A token can be:
- A word
- A sentence
- A punctuation mark
- Sometimes a sub-word

Example:
Text: "I love Python."
Word tokens: ["I", "love", "Python", "."]

Why is it used?
Computers need text to be converted into smaller units before many NLP tasks can be performed.

Common types:
- Sentence tokenization: breaks a paragraph into sentences.
- Word tokenization: breaks a sentence into words.

Simple idea:
Text -> Tokens -> Further NLP processing


2. POS TAGGING
--------------
POS stands for Part-of-Speech.

POS tagging assigns a grammatical category to each word, such as:
- Noun: person, place, thing
- Verb: action
- Adjective: describes a noun
- Adverb: describes an action or adjective
- Pronoun: replaces a noun
- Preposition: shows relationship between words

Example:
"Python is powerful."

Python -> Noun
is -> Verb
powerful -> Adjective

Why is it used?
POS tagging helps a computer understand the grammatical role of words in a sentence.


3. STOPWORDS
------------
Stopwords are common words that often carry little useful information for some NLP tasks.

Examples:
- the
- is
- a
- an
- of
- in
- and
- to

Example:
Original: "The cat is sitting on the table."
After removing common stopwords:
"cat sitting table"

Why remove them?
Removing stopwords can reduce unnecessary text and make some NLP tasks faster and simpler.

Important:
Stopwords should not always be removed. In some tasks, words such as "not" are important for meaning and sentiment.


4. WORD FREQUENCY ANALYSIS
--------------------------
Word frequency analysis counts how many times each word appears in a text.

Example:
Text: "Python is easy. Python is powerful."

Frequency:
Python -> 2
is -> 2
easy -> 1
powerful -> 1

Why is it used?
It helps identify:
- Common words
- Important repeated terms
- Main topics in a document
- Patterns in text

A frequency table or bar chart can be used to visualize the most common words.


5. NER (NAMED ENTITY RECOGNITION)
---------------------------------
NER stands for Named Entity Recognition.

It identifies important real-world entities in text and gives them categories.

Common entity types:
- PERSON -> a person's name
- ORGANIZATION -> company or organization
- LOCATION/GPE -> place or country
- DATE -> date
- MONEY -> monetary value

Example:
"Elon Musk founded SpaceX in 2002."

Elon Musk -> PERSON
SpaceX -> ORGANIZATION
2002 -> DATE

Why is it used?
NER helps computers find useful information such as people, organizations, locations, and dates from text.


6. SENTIMENT ANALYSIS USING POLARITY
------------------------------------
Sentiment analysis determines the emotional opinion expressed in text.

Polarity usually represents whether the text is:
- Positive
- Negative
- Neutral

A common polarity score ranges from -1 to +1.

General interpretation:
- Close to +1 -> Positive
- Close to 0 -> Neutral
- Close to -1 -> Negative

Example:
"I love this product!"
Polarity -> Positive

"This product is terrible."
Polarity -> Negative

"The product arrived today."
Polarity -> Neutral

Why is it used?
It can be used for:
- Product reviews
- Customer feedback
- Social media analysis
- Opinion mining


7. TF-IDF
---------
TF-IDF stands for Term Frequency-Inverse Document Frequency.

It is a technique used to represent text numerically and identify words that are important in a document.

TF (Term Frequency):
Measures how often a word appears in a document.

IDF (Inverse Document Frequency):
Measures how uncommon a word is across a collection of documents.

Basic idea:
- A word that appears often in one document can be important.
- A word that appears in almost every document is usually less useful for distinguishing documents.

Example:
If the word "machine-learning" appears many times in one document but rarely in other documents, TF-IDF can give it a relatively high score.

Why is it used?
TF-IDF is commonly used for:
- Text classification
- Document similarity
- Search systems
- Keyword extraction
- Resume-job matching

Important:
TF-IDF produces numerical vectors, but these vectors are not semantic embeddings. Similarity is based mainly on word usage.


8. BAG OF WORDS (BOW)
---------------------
Bag of Words is a simple method for converting text into numbers.

It creates a vocabulary of words and records how many times each word appears.

Example:
Documents:
D1: "I like Python"
D2: "I like Java"

Vocabulary:
["I", "like", "Python", "Java"]

Vectors:
D1 -> [1, 1, 1, 0]
D2 -> [1, 1, 0, 1]

The order of words is ignored.

Why is it used?
BOW is useful for simple NLP tasks such as:
- Text classification
- Spam detection
- Document comparison
- Basic machine learning models

Limitation:
BOW does not understand the meaning or context of words.


9. EMBEDDINGS
-------------
Embeddings are numerical representations of text.

They represent words, sentences, or documents as vectors of numbers.

The main idea is that text with similar meanings can have similar vector representations.

Example:
"king" and "queen" may have related vector representations because their meanings are related.

Sentence example:
"The cat is sleeping."
"The kitten is resting."

A good embedding model can represent these sentences as vectors that are relatively similar because their meanings are related.

Why are embeddings used?
They are widely used for:
- Semantic search
- Recommendation systems
- Text similarity
- RAG systems
- Question answering
- Classification
- LLM applications

Difference from BOW and TF-IDF:
- BOW mainly counts words.
- TF-IDF gives importance to words based on frequency.
- Embeddings aim to represent semantic meaning and relationships.


QUICK COMPARISON
----------------

Tokenization:
Breaks text into smaller units.

POS Tagging:
Identifies the grammatical role of words.

Stopwords:
Identifies/removes common words when appropriate.

Word Frequency:
Counts how often words appear.

NER:
Finds named entities such as people, organizations, and places.

Sentiment Analysis:
Determines whether text is positive, negative, or neutral.

BOW:
Represents text using word counts.

TF-IDF:
Represents text using word importance.

Embeddings:
Represents text as vectors that can capture semantic relationships.

------------------
A simple NLP workflow can look like:

Raw Text
   |
   v
Tokenization
   |
   v
Stopword Handling / Text Cleaning
   |
   v
POS Tagging / NER
   |
   v
Feature Representation
   |
   +----> BOW
   |
   +----> TF-IDF
   |
   +----> Embeddings
   |
   v
Machine Learning / NLP Task
   |
   +----> Classification
   +----> Sentiment Analysis
   +----> Similarity
   +----> Search
   +----> Question Answering


FINAL SUMMARY
-------------
NLP allows computers to work with human language.

The topics covered today form important building blocks:

1. Tokenization -> breaks text into manageable pieces.
2. POS Tagging -> identifies word roles.
3. Stopwords -> handles very common words.
4. Word Frequency -> counts repeated words.
5. NER -> identifies real-world entities.
6. Sentiment Analysis -> identifies opinion/polarity.
7. BOW -> converts text into word-count vectors.
8. TF-IDF -> converts text into importance-based vectors.
9. Embeddings -> converts text into meaningful numerical vectors.

Together, these techniques help transform raw human language into information that computers and machine learning models can process.
